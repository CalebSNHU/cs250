How do I interpret user needs and implement them into a program? How does creating “user stories” help with this?

While it's important to also consider the letter of a user's words when it comes to complaints or requests, often the real work is in reading between the lines to their underlying need and being able to find the best solution to that need. If a customer says "I don't like my web site" then my job is not to write that into a story and turn the "customer happiness" knob on the web server, but to follow up and ask them "why?" Maybe the problem is the way it looks, maybe it loads too slowly, maybe it's not designed well for mobile or any number of other possible issues, but if I just guess then chances are good that they won't be happy with whatever I produce. At the same time, if they thought it was too slow, and also want to add a bunch of flashy animations and heavy videos to it, then solving the second problem is probably going to make the first one worse. I need to be able to look at their requests and their existing software holistically and help them to understand their options before I start laying out stories. The stories themselves help to keep all of this information organized, and build a clear plan of how to address those issues for the rest of the Agile team.

What does it mean to be a good team member in software development?

You could qualify it a thousand ways, but I think the most important part of being a good team member in software development is to have a sense of ownership of the product, and do whatever you can to ensure that both the application and the team are given consideration and respect. Often these things go hand in hand. Someone who doesn't care about their code, also doesn't care about the mess that they are leaving in the codebase for someone else to come clean up later. They aren't diseminationg information to their peers, and they aren't taking responsibility for the customer experience. It puts a burden on everyone else on the team, and the consequences of those choices can have a  permenant impact on the team and the product.
